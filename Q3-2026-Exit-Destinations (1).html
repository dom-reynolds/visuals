Exit Destination Four-Week Series

From July 1 to September 30, 863 clients exited our projects. Over the next four weeks we will share where clients went after exit, starting with an overview, then destination by category, housing subsidies, and destination by race and ethnicity. Acronyms are defined in the Acronym Index at the bottom of each week.

Week 1 of 4

What is Exit Destination?

Exit destination is where a client goes when they leave one of our projects. Staff ask each client where they will be staying and record the answer in HMIS at exit.

Why is Exit Destination Important?

Exit destination tells us what happened to clients after they left our care. The number of clients served shows how many people we reached, while exit destination shows whether those clients moved into housing, returned to shelter or the street, or left without telling us where they were going.

How Does Exit Destination Inform HUD Funding to our Community?

We report exit destination to HUD through the Annual Performance Report (APR), questions Q23c through Q23e. The same data is used for HUD System Performance Measure 7 (SPM 7), which measures how many clients are placed in or stay in permanent housing. HUD uses SPM 7 when scoring our community’s CoC application and when making ESG funding decisions, so our exit outcomes directly affect the federal dollars that come to our community.

How Do We Use This Data?

Breaking exit destination down by household type, destination category, subsidy type, and race and ethnicity helps us see which clients are reaching stable housing and where more support is needed.

What Counts as a Positive Exit?

Positive

Permanent Housing

Renting or owning, with or without a subsidy, or staying with family or friends with permanent tenure. For Street Outreach (SO) projects, HUD also counts exits to ES, TH, other temporary destinations, and some institutional settings as positive.

Not positive

All Other Destinations

Emergency shelter, places not meant for habitation, temporary stays, and most institutional settings. HUD also counts No exit interview completed, Client doesn’t know/prefers not to answer, Data not collected, and Other as not positive.

Excluded from the rate

Excluded Destinations

  • Deceased
  • Foster care home or foster care group home
  • Hospital or other residential non-psychiatric medical facility
  • Long-term care facility or nursing home

Week 2 of 4

Where Did Clients Go After Exit?

APR Q23c groups more than 30 HUD destinations into five categories: Permanent, Temporary, Institutional, Homeless, and Other. This week looks at how exits were spread across those categories and which destinations made up each one.

How Did Destination Differ by Household Type? Q23c

Share of exits in each category. Households with children and adults exited to PH more often than households without children, and also made up most exits to a place not meant for habitation.

Key Takeaways

294 Rentals With No Ongoing Subsidy

This was the most common destination, making up 34% of all exits and 65% of permanent exits. 214 of these were households with children and adults.

47 Clients in Families Exited Unsheltered

47 clients in households with children and adults exited to a place not meant for habitation. That is 77% of the 61 unsheltered exits, while these households made up 60% of all exits.

80 Clients Staying With Family Temporarily

This was the most common temporary destination. These clients have somewhere to stay, but without permanent tenure.

171 Exits With No Known Destination

19.8% of exits were No exit interview completed, Other, Client doesn’t know/prefers not to answer, or Data not collected. See the data flag below.

Data flag

Other, Unknown, and Data Not Collected Q23c

175 exits fall under Other Situations in Q23c. Four of these are deceased, which HUD excludes from the rate. The remaining 171 (19.8% of all exits) do not have a known destination and are counted as not positive.

Why Does This Happen?

  • No exit interview completed (81): Clients may leave ES or SO without notice, stop coming, or cannot be reached after they leave. 60 of the 81 are households without children.
  • Other (52): Used when a client’s destination does not match one of the HUD options. 44 of the 52 are households with children and adults.
  • Client doesn’t know/prefers not to answer (28): Some clients do not have a plan yet at exit, or choose not to share it.
  • Data not collected (10): The destination was left blank in HMIS.

Why Does It Matter?

  • HUD counts each of these as not positive in the APR and SPM 7, the same as an exit to a place not meant for habitation.
  • An unknown destination does not mean the client is unhoused. Some of these clients may have moved into housing we were not told about.
  • Because of this, our 54.99% rate should be read as a minimum.

Why Do We Still Report It?

  • We follow HUD’s reporting rules so our numbers match what we submit to HUD and can be compared with other providers.
  • Reporting these exits shows our funders how much of the outcome data is still unknown.
  • Completing exit interviews at last contact, making follow-up calls, and reviewing Other entries can reduce the number of unknown destinations.
